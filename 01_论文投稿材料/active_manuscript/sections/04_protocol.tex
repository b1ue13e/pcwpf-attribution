\section{Scoping Audit Protocol and Empirical Test Bench}
\label{sec:protocol}

We conducted an empirical scoping audit in accordance with the PRISMA Extension for Scoping Reviews (PRISMA-ScR) guidelines \cite{tricco2018} and established scoping methodology \cite{levac2010}. The protocol was specified prior to full-text extraction to govern retrieval queries, eligibility thresholds, screening procedures, and inter-rater reliability arbitration.

\subsection{Information Sources and Retrieval Strategy}
We retrieved candidate literature across two complementary discovery channels covering January 1, 2023, through March 1, 2026, with rolling monitoring through July 2026 for emergent preprints. First, we conducted programmatic searches via the OpenAlex API, which indexes records across IEEE Xplore, ScienceDirect, SpringerLink, Wiley, and MDPI. Using four targeted title queries---$Q_1$ (\texttt{"physics-constrained" AND "wind"}), $Q_2$ (\texttt{"physics-informed" AND "wind power"}), $Q_3$ (\texttt{"physics-guided" AND "wind"}), and $Q_4$ (\texttt{"physics-embedded" AND "wind power"})---we identified 72 candidate records. Second, we conducted targeted searches on Google Scholar, arXiv, and preprint servers to capture early-access articles and working papers, yielding 17 additional records ($N=89$ total). Candidate records were cross-checked against Scopus and IEEE Xplore to verify coverage of major power systems and applied energy venues. 

To assess search sensitivity, a targeted query expanding retrieval to title, abstract, and author keywords across Scopus and IEEE Xplore (encompassing broader descriptors such as \texttt{"knowledge-guided"}, \texttt{"hybrid"}, and \texttt{"domain-informed"}) identified 14 additional papers. Full-text review confirmed that none documented operational failure boundaries or negative trade-offs; the same reporting pattern was observed in the 14-study sensitivity sample, with zero studies reporting operational failure regimes. We emphasize that this 14-study sensitivity query served strictly as an external check on negative trade-off reporting; exhaustive five-dimensional extraction ($\mathcal{D}_1$--$\mathcal{D}_5$) and inter-rater reliability coding were conducted strictly on the core PRISMA-ScR scoping cohort of 46 studies to prevent cohort drift.

\subsection{Screening, Deduplication, and Eligibility Criteria}
Candidate records underwent structured multi-phase screening following the PRISMA-ScR workflow (Figure~\ref{fig:prisma_flow}). Cross-source deduplication first eliminated 17 duplicate records from the initial 89 items, leaving 72 unique entries for title and abstract evaluation. Screening at this stage excluded 19 records with documented rationale: nine addressed non-forecasting tasks such as turbine structural health monitoring, blade crack detection, or aeroelastic fatigue modeling; nine focused on real-time blade pitch or active power control rather than multi-step power forecasting; and one contained no physics-based modeling components. This filtering yielded 53 candidate studies sought for full-text retrieval.

Because authors frequently release preliminary working papers on preprint servers (such as arXiv or SSRN) prior to formal journal publication, treating preprints and published articles independently would artificially inflate corpus statistics. We enforced a strict study-level deduplication rule: whenever a preprint and a peer-reviewed journal paper shared identical authorship, empirical datasets, and mathematical formulations, they were merged into a single study-level record retaining the peer-reviewed journal citation. Five paired duplicate sets were identified and consolidated, reducing the candidate cohort from 53 to 48 unique study-level entries.

The remaining 48 full texts were independently evaluated against three explicit scoping eligibility criteria: (1) primary focus on short-term or ultra-short-term wind power forecasting; (2) mathematical formulation and computational execution of an explicit physical constraint, governing equation, or domain envelope; and (3) empirical validation on real-world or simulated wind power generation time series. Two studies failed to meet these criteria: study S26 was excluded because it forecasted atmospheric wind velocity exclusively without an explicit turbine power constraint, while study S42 was excluded because it implemented heuristic feature clustering without domain physical equations. This systematic filtering established our final representative scoping corpus of \textbf{$N=46$ unique scoping studies}, comprising 38 peer-reviewed journal articles and eight representative preprints (including UniWind \cite{s27_uniwind2026}).

\subsection{Multi-Rater Reliability Benchmarking}
To assess the empirical stability of the taxonomy before full extraction, three independent raters (Coder A, Coder B, and Coder C) double-blindly coded a stratified representative subset of 20 candidate studies across all five dimensions. Study eligibility screening achieved unanimous consensus (Fleiss $\kappa = 1.000$), correctly isolating negative controls S26 and S42. Parameter provenance ($\mathcal{D}_1$) demonstrated perfect agreement ($\kappa = 1.000$), while application stage ($\mathcal{D}_2$) yielded near-perfect agreement ($\kappa = 0.950$ overall, $\kappa = 0.938$ on included studies). Inference telemetry requirements ($\mathcal{D}_3$) achieved substantial agreement ($\kappa = 0.743$ overall, $\kappa = 0.646$ on included studies).

Implementation fidelity ($\mathcal{D}_4$) exhibited moderate concordance prior to consensus arbitration ($\kappa = 0.716$ overall in the stratified pilot, but $\kappa = 0.341$ when evaluated strictly on included studies). This reduction reflects the classical prevalence paradox described by Feinstein and Cicchetti: because 95.7\% of candidate studies omitted public code, expected chance agreement was high, which depresses Fleiss' $\kappa$ despite a raw observed agreement of 90.7\%. Evaluating static manuscripts without code repositories leaves coders to interpret ambiguous narrative descriptions regarding whether a constraint represents an active computational component or conceptual motivation. This divergence indicates that software implementation fidelity cannot be reliably inferred from published prose alone, motivating direct computational graph inspection. For ablation semantics ($\mathcal{D}_5$), raters achieved strong concordance ($\kappa = 0.851$ overall, $\kappa = 0.818$ on included studies). On harm reporting, all three raters unanimously coded zero instances of harm documentation across the included pilot studies ($0/18$); because marginal variance was zero, Cohen's $\kappa$ is mathematically indeterminate and is formally reported as 100\% observed agreement ($P_o = 1.000$). Following pilot calibration and consensus arbitration, full-text extraction across the complete 46-study corpus was executed at the study level.

\subsection{Software Audit Protocol (PCT \& PINN-UQ)}
The code availability audit followed a standardized software search protocol examining full-text bodies, footnotes, data availability statements, publisher repositories, and author profiles. Across the 46-study cohort, only two publicly accessible software repositories were identified. One repository, PINN-UQ (S10, \textit{Eng. Appl. Artif. Intell.} 2025), was audited and verified to faithfully implement its specified algebraic power equations ($P = g T \omega$ and $P = \frac{1}{2} C_p \rho A v^3$ via \texttt{sn.Tie}); its computational graph strictly executes the published electromechanical relationships without unstated differential substitutions, earning classification as \textit{code-aligned}. The remaining repository, the Physics-Constrained Transformer (PCT, S02, \textit{Sci. Rep.} 2026), provided open PyTorch scripts (\texttt{train\_clean.py} and \texttt{tst/loss.py}) which we audited line-by-line for parity between published loss formulations and computational graph execution.

\subsection{Downstream Operational Dispatch and Settlement Test Bench}
To ground the empirical audit in real-world power system operations across diverse meteorological and topographic terrains, we established an operational test bench across three commercial UK wind farms with 10-minute SCADA telemetry. The \textit{Hill of Towie Wind Farm} (48.3~MW rated capacity, 21 Siemens SWT-2.3-82 turbines) features complex upland terrain and serves as an independent spatiotemporal extrapolation benchmark (7,599 test origins, 45,594 delivery steps across full-year 2017), with data released by RES on behalf of TRIG openly available on Zenodo under CC-BY-4.0 \cite{zenodo_hilloftowie}. The \textit{Kelmarsh Wind Farm} (12.3~MW rated capacity, six Senvion MM92 turbines) is situated in flat agricultural terrain and serves as our primary development benchmark (8,212 test origins, 49,272 delivery steps across full-year 2017) \cite{zenodo_kelmarsh}. The \textit{Penmanshiel Wind Farm} (28.7~MW rated capacity, 14 Senvion MM82 turbines) is located in the Scottish Borders \cite{zenodo_penmanshiel}, providing an independent upland setting with 8,440 test origins configured with a restricted training partition ($N_{\text{train}} = 1,583$) to evaluate model behavior under sample-restricted regimes.

The empirical test bench operates under a prospective evaluation protocol. The forecasting pipeline adopts an input lookback of $L=36$ (6 hours) and a forecast horizon of $H=36$ (6 hours), with downstream economic dispatch evaluated over the next-hour delivery window $H_{\text{disp}} = \{1, \dots, 6\}$ and an origination stride of $S=6$ steps to prevent overlapping settlement intervals. The core model suite spans unconstrained DLinear, Bounds-Constrained DLinear ($\lambda=0.01$), Empirical Power Curve Constrained DLinear ($\lambda \in \{0.01, 0.1, 1.0\}$), and Conditional Distribution Prior DLinear ($\lambda \in \{0.01, 0.1, 1.0\}$), benchmarked alongside Ridge regression and Persistence baselines. Penalty schedules $\lambda \in \{0.01, 0.1, 1.0\}$ were specified as a predefined sensitivity and stress-testing grid prior to evaluation: $\lambda=0.01$ evaluates weak numerical regularization, $\lambda=0.1$ evaluates moderate constraint enforcement, and $\lambda=1.0$ evaluates strong stress testing for prior specificity. Hyperparameters and model checkpoint selection were governed exclusively on the 2016 validation partition; the 2017 test year was accessed only after model architectures and hyperparameter regimes were frozen. No 2017 test observations were used for hyperparameter or checkpoint selection in these runs. A reviewer-motivated follow-up robustness experiment was subsequently conducted under matched sample windows with the model architecture, $\lambda$ grid, and 2016-based calibration protocol held fixed. To verify whether findings extend beyond linear representations, we evaluate a multi-patch channel-independent Transformer backbone (\textbf{PatchTST} \cite{nie2023patchtst}, patch length 6, stride 4, $d_{\text{model}}=64$, 2-layer encoder with RevIN) across all three wind farms, four constraint classes, and five random seeds (60 trained models) as an architectural sensitivity probe under nominal regularization ($\lambda=0.01$).

Downstream decision calibration is implemented via a post-hoc quantile offset $\delta_h$ estimated strictly from the independent 2016 calibration window ($q = 1/(r+1) = 1/3$ for penalty ratio $r=2$) with zero access to 2017 test telemetry or market prices. Real-world balancing price dynamics are evaluated using official 2017 UK Elexon BSC System Buy Prices (mean \pounds 44.27/MWh, peak \pounds 1,509.80/MWh, with 68 negative price periods down to -\pounds 73.15/MWh). In Great Britain, wholesale electricity is traded bilaterally and on voluntary power exchanges; post-gate closure, the Balancing and Settlement Code (BSC) calculates energy imbalance cashflows under single-price cash-out rules (post-P305) by comparing a party's contracted volume (notified via Energy Contract Volume Notifications, ECVN) against metered physical volume. Physical Notifications (such as Final Physical Notifications, FPN) represent operational generation profiles submitted to the transmission system operator for physical system balancing, distinct from contractual settlement volumes. To capture realistic commercial accounting without conflating standalone imbalance cashflow with net profit, we formulate a stylized two-component accounting model \cite{pinson2007trading} coupling forward commitments and physical imbalance settlement:
\begin{equation}
R_{\text{total}} = P_{\text{F}} \cdot q + P_{\text{imb}} \cdot (y - q),
\label{eq:two_settlement}
\end{equation}
where $P_{\text{F}}$ denotes the forward contract price, $q$ is the forward-contracted or nominated energy position in the stylized model, $y$ is actual physical generation, and $P_{\text{imb}}$ is the BSC system imbalance cash-out price. Modelled cashflows incorporate a half-hour aggregation of the modelled imbalance exposure:
\begin{equation}
E_{\text{SP}} = \sum_{t \in \text{SP}} (y_t - q_t) P_{\text{rated}} \Delta t \quad [\text{MWh}], \quad \Delta t = \frac{1}{6}\text{ h},
\label{eq:bsc_netting}
\end{equation}
where $P_{\text{rated}}$ denotes total wind farm nameplate capacity in MW (12.3~MW for Kelmarsh, 48.3~MW for Hill of Towie, 28.7~MW for Penmanshiel). Farm-level cashflows were divided by the number of turbines at each site ($N_{\text{turb}} \in \{6, 21, 14\}$) and annualized to report \pounds/(turb$\cdot$yr), generating a stylized BSC-price-based imbalance cashflow diagnostic that abstracts from account-level contractual notifications, Balancing Mechanism acceptances, and residual system cashflow reallocation, validated via 2,000-replication 7-day circular block paired bootstrapping.
