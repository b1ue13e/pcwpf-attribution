\section{Empirical Audit Results}
\label{sec:results}

Empirical evaluation across the 46-study scoping cohort and three operational wind farms adjudicates the attributional foundation and operational performance of physics-constrained wind power forecasting.

\subsection{Reporting Patterns and Code Availability in the Audited Scoping Cohort}
Across the core 46-study scoping cohort (2023--2026), zero studies reported operational failure regimes or negative trade-offs (0/46, 0.0\%; 95\% Wilson CI: [0.0\%, 7.7\%]). A targeted 14-study sensitivity search expanding query terms across Scopus and IEEE Xplore likewise identified zero negative trade-off reports, verifying that this reporting pattern is robust to broader search strings. Furthermore, only three studies in the core cohort (6.5\%; S01, S02, S10) evaluated sensitivity to prior parameter perturbations; the remaining 43 studies (93.5\%) tested constraints exclusively under nominal design configurations. Restricting empirical evaluations to nominal conditions obscures operational failure boundaries and substitutes uniform improvement claims for engineering trade-offs.

Software availability inspection reveals an open-code deficit of 95.7\% (44/46, 95\% Wilson CI: [85.5\%, 98.8\%], $\mathcal{D}_4 = \texttt{not-found}$), with 44 studies providing neither executable code repositories nor reproducible scripts. Among the two studies with accessible code, PINN-UQ (S10) faithfully executes its published electromechanical tie equations ($\mathcal{D}_4 = \texttt{code-aligned}$), whereas the Physics-Constrained Transformer (PCT, S02) exhibits a directional implementation divergence in its divergence loss formulation ($\mathcal{D}_4 = \texttt{code-mismatch}$, detailed in Section~\ref{sec:code_audit}).

Ablation semantic audit ($\mathcal{D}_5$) reveals that half of the audited studies (23/46, 50.0\%, 95\% Wilson CI: [36.1\%, 63.9\%]) evaluate constraints by excising entire multi-layer neural submodules---such as graph convolutions, cross-attention blocks, or decomposition layers---rather than toggling the constraint penalty multiplier ($\lambda=0$ vs.\ $\lambda>0$; Table~\ref{tab:lifecycle_distribution}, Figure~\ref{fig:audit_distribution}). Because excising architectural blocks removes thousands of trainable parameters simultaneously, reported performance gains reflect representational capacity rather than aerodynamic guidance. These findings describe the audited 46-study scoping cohort under Wilson confidence bounds without asserting universal extrapolation to unbounded keyword taxonomies.

Table~\ref{tab:controlled_ablation} and Figure~\ref{fig:causal_ablation} reveal four empirical boundary conditions:

\paragraph{Defensive Inductive Bias and Sensitivity to Prior Misspecification (Figures~\ref{fig:causal_ablation}a, \ref{fig:within_site_learning_curve}, and \ref{fig:corruption_gradient})}
On Penmanshiel under low-sample training ($N_{\text{train}} = 1,583$), unconstrained DLinear exhibits empirical over-fitting ($0.09881 \pm 0.00027$). Embedding the authentic empirical power curve at $\lambda=1.0$ reduces normalized dispatch cost to $0.09760 \pm 0.00044$ ($-1.22\%$). In contrast, the scale-matched Pseudo-Scrambled negative control inflates dispatch cost to $0.16611 \pm 0.00335$ ($+68.1\%$). This contrast demonstrates that in low-sample regimes, neural forecasters can benefit from valid aerodynamic guidance, but are vulnerable to prior misspecification under strong regularization.

To decouple sample volume from topography, we evaluated within-site learning curves across $N_{\text{train}} \in \{500, 1000, 1500, 3000, 5000\}$ on Kelmarsh and Hill of Towie across five random seeds (Figure~\ref{fig:within_site_learning_curve}). Under restricted training windows ($N_{\text{train}} = 500$, representing $\approx 21$ days of recent operational history), authentic physical priors consistently lower dispatch costs across both wind farms. On Kelmarsh, dispatch cost falls from $0.09648 \pm 0.00314$ (unconstrained) to $0.09455 \pm 0.00285$ ($-2.00\%$), while scrambled priors surge to $0.13798 \pm 0.02338$ ($+43.0\%$). On Hill of Towie, authentic priors reduce cost from $0.10444 \pm 0.00312$ to $0.10066 \pm 0.00307$ ($-3.62\%$), whereas scrambled priors escalate to $0.17209 \pm 0.01703$ ($+64.8\%$). Importantly, the empirical prior is fitted once on the entire pre-cutoff training period (7 months for Kelmarsh, 10 months for Hill of Towie), providing an auxiliary seasonal data budget that assists restricted model training windows. As model training volume expands to $N_{\text{train}} = 5{,}000$, unconstrained DLinear achieves $0.08812$ on Kelmarsh and $0.08936$ on Hill of Towie, closing the gap and slightly outperforming the fixed genuine prior ($0.08841$, $+0.33\%$ on Kelmarsh; $0.09091$, $+1.73\%$ on Hill of Towie). In contrast, scrambled priors remain severely detrimental ($+43\%$ to $+98\%$) across all sample sizes. These findings indicate that while authentic empirical priors provide downside protection in data-scarce windows, their relative advantage diminishes as training volume expands.

Evaluating continuous corruption levels $\alpha \in [0.0, 1.0]$ demonstrates that prior failure is not an artifact of total permutation (Figure~\ref{fig:corruption_gradient}a). On Penmanshiel, dispatch cost degrades monotonically with corruption, crossing the unconstrained baseline around $\alpha \approx 0.07$ (at $\alpha=0.2$, dispatch cost reaches $0.10469 \pm 0.00338$, a $+5.9\%$ degradation). Evaluating engineering drift reveals asymmetrical operational tolerances: aerodynamic aging exceeding $10\%$ rated capacity derating (Figure~\ref{fig:corruption_gradient}b) flips nominal physical priors into economic penalties. For anemometer calibration, Penmanshiel maintains a modest advantage over unconstrained models under positive sensor biases up to $+15\%$ ($0.09833$ vs.\ $0.09881$, Figure~\ref{fig:corruption_gradient}c), whereas negative wind speed under-reporting systematically inflates shortfall.

\paragraph{Low-$\lambda$ Numerical Regularization Mimicry (Figure~\ref{fig:causal_ablation}b)}
At weak regularization ($\lambda = 0.01$), genuine physical priors and pseudo-scrambled controls yield closely matched dispatch costs on Kelmarsh ($0.08810 \pm 0.00009$ vs.\ $0.08801 \pm 0.00008$). Because random power curve permutations produce outcomes comparable to genuine aerodynamics at this weak penalty scale, marginal performance improvements reported under weak penalties cannot be specifically attributed to aerodynamic reasoning and remain consistent with non-specific numerical smoothing.

\paragraph{Sensitivity to Training-Loss Physical Proxy Staleness (Figure~\ref{fig:causal_ablation}c)}
Parametric scanning of historical wind speed proxy lag $k \in \{6, 12, 18, 36\}$ (evaluating 1\,h to 6\,h operational delay in the training-loss proxy query) produces strictly monotonic cost inflation across all three farms (Hill of Towie: from $0.09022$ at $k=6$ to $0.09257$ at $k=36$, exceeding unconstrained $0.08924$). We emphasize that this experiment isolates the sensitivity of the training objective to temporal misalignment between the forecast horizon and the historical wind speed proxy used to index the power curve. It does not represent an inference-time telemetry delay, as the forecaster takes only past power measurements during test-time inference. However, it demonstrates that if an offline training loss regularizes predictions against lagged weather observations without forward meteorological forecasts, proxy staleness degrades the resulting inductive guidance.

\paragraph{Information-Symmetric Feature Baseline}
Providing the unconstrained model with contemporaneous wind speed (\textit{Dual-Channel DLinear}) reduces point forecast RMSE (from $0.1717$ to $0.1697$ on Kelmarsh) but raises tail dispatch risk ($0.08910$ vs.\ $0.08812$), because unhedged wind speed fluctuations inflate bidding variance.

\subsubsection{Primary Inferential Contrasts and Metric Framework}
To control for multiplicity across operational dimensions, our evaluation pipeline specifies three \textbf{primary inferential contrasts} evaluated under normalized static dispatch cost:
(1)~\textit{Genuine Physical Prior vs.\ Unconstrained Baseline under sample restriction} ($N_{\text{train}}=1{,}583$ on Penmanshiel), confirming downside risk protection ($\Delta = -1.22\%$, $0.09760 \pm 0.00044$ vs.\ $0.09881 \pm 0.00027$);
(2)~\textit{Scrambled Prior vs.\ Unconstrained Baseline under strong regularization} ($\lambda=1.0$), demonstrating misspecification vulnerability ($\Delta = +68.1\%$, $0.16611 \pm 0.00335$ vs.\ $0.09881 \pm 0.00027$); and
(3)~\textit{Genuine vs.\ Scrambled Prior under weak regularization} ($\lambda=0.01$), confirming indistinguishable behavior consistent with non-specific numerical smoothing ($0.08810 \pm 0.00009$ vs.\ $0.08801 \pm 0.00008$).
Exploratory dynamic price comparisons (Table~\ref{tab:paired_bootstrap}) evaluate dynamic cost divergences on Hill of Towie and Kelmarsh under paired circular block bootstrapping. Methodologically, our evaluation protocol separates two distinct sources of variation: the paired bootstrap intervals in Table~\ref{tab:paired_bootstrap} estimate \textbf{temporal sampling uncertainty} across delivery periods under meteorological autocorrelation, whereas the reported metric margins ($\text{mean} \pm \text{std}$) across random seeds in Table~\ref{tab:controlled_ablation} characterize \textbf{model initialization and optimization variability}.

\subsection{Decoupled Decision Risk and Single-Settlement Imbalance Diagnostic (The Zero-Bid Anomaly)}
Evaluating forecasters on isolated unhedged balancing market cashflows introduces a structural evaluation pitfall: under 2017 UK BSC 30-minute netting, a degenerate Zero-Bid baseline ($\hat{y} \equiv 0$) extracts \pounds 223k to \pounds 243k per turbine-year in apparent cashflow without generating forecasts (Table~\ref{tab:zero_bid_diagnostic}, Figure~\ref{fig:market_decoupling}). This analysis serves as a \textit{decision-risk and imbalance-settlement diagnostic} rather than a commercial market valuation, exposing metric pathologies in downstream evaluation claims.

Table~\ref{tab:zero_bid_diagnostic} and Figure~\ref{fig:market_decoupling} uncover two fundamental evaluation pitfalls:

\paragraph{Degenerate Zero-Bid Settlement Anomaly and Two-Settlement Accounting (Figure~\ref{fig:market_decoupling}a)}
A degenerate forecaster bidding zero in every settlement period incurs \textbf{\pounds 0.00 shortfall penalty} and collects an apparent balancing cashflow of \textbf{+\pounds 223k to +\pounds 243k per turbine-year} across all three wind farms. In standard two-settlement wholesale accounting \cite{pinson2007trading}, total commercial revenue couples forward commitments with real-time imbalance settlement:
\begin{equation}
R_{\text{total}} = P_{\text{F}} \cdot q + P_{\text{imb}} \cdot (y - q),
\label{eq:two_settlement_disc}
\end{equation}
where $q$ denotes the declared forward commitment, $P_{\text{F}}$ is the forward contract price, $y$ is actual physical generation volume, and $P_{\text{imb}}$ is the system imbalance cash-out price (under single-price settlement post-P305 in the UK BSC, $P_{\text{SBP}} \approx P_{\text{SSP}} = P_{\text{imb}}$). When downstream evaluations isolate balancing cashflows $R_{\text{imb}} = P_{\text{imb}} \cdot (y - q)$ from forward market revenue $P_{\text{F}} \cdot q$, setting $q \equiv 0$ converts total generation $y$ into uncontracted spill credited at $P_{\text{imb}}$, producing an apparent six-figure revenue while forfeiting forward market receipts. The Zero-Bid baseline acts as an empirical \textit{reductio ad absurdum}: standalone unhedged balancing cashflow is structurally degenerate when disconnected from forward commitments, confirming that unhedged single-settlement accounting cannot serve as an index of forecast quality.

Under asymmetric operational loss ($r=2, q=1/3$), the Zero-Bid strategy yields a Decision Cost of \textbf{$0.33689$} on Penmanshiel (Table~\ref{tab:zero_bid_diagnostic}), correctly penalizing the policy for severe under-commitment ($\text{Cost}_{\text{static}} = \bar{y} \approx 0.337$, matching the site's mean capacity factor). In contrast to unhedged single-settlement cashflow, operational loss metrics penalize persistent non-delivery.

\paragraph{Empirical Metric Inversion on Hill of Towie (Figure~\ref{fig:market_decoupling}b)}
On Hill of Towie, distribution regularization increases nominal balancing cashflow by $+42.5\%$ (\pounds 8,543 to \pounds 12,174/(turb$\cdot$yr), Table~\ref{tab:zero_bid_diagnostic}) while concurrently degrading normalized decision risk ($\text{Cost}_{\text{static}}$) by \textbf{$+6.5\%$} and RMSE by $+3.5\%$. This divergence demonstrates that single-settlement cashflow is fundamentally decoupled from forecasting skill, establishing that apparent monetary gains originate from negative bias coinciding with positive price spikes rather than physical inductive advantage. Operational evaluation requires decoupling non-negative asymmetric decision risk (Pinball loss) from historical monetary settlement diagnostics.

\subsection{Cross-Backbone Architectural Sensitivity Probe (PatchTST)}
To assess whether empirical boundary behaviors extend beyond linear representations without asserting universal inductive invariance, we evaluated a state-of-the-art multi-patch channel-independent Transformer (\textbf{PatchTST} \cite{nie2023patchtst}, patch length 6, stride 4, 2-layer encoder with RevIN) across 60 independently trained models (3 farms $\times$ 4 constraint conditions $\times$ 5 seeds; Table~\ref{tab:patchtst_and_rules}). This setup functions strictly as an \textit{architectural sensitivity probe} under fixed nominal regularization ($\lambda=0.01$) matched to the primary development budget, intentionally bounded to qualitative directional verification rather than an exhaustive cross-backbone hyperparameter search.

\input{tables/table5_patchtst_and_rules.tex}

As detailed in Table~\ref{tab:patchtst_and_rules}, PatchTST confirms that distribution priors reduce dynamic shortfall across all three wind farms (saving \pounds 232.44, \pounds 127.49, and \pounds 790.51/(turb$\cdot$yr) on Hill of Towie, Kelmarsh, and Penmanshiel, respectively), indicating that the defensive shortfall reduction observed under DLinear persists in non-linear patch-based representations. However, net balancing cashflow diverges sharply between architectures: distribution regularization decreases net cashflow on Hill of Towie by $-\text{\pounds} 568.68$/(turb$\cdot$yr) under PatchTST, inverting the positive cashflow effect observed under DLinear. On Penmanshiel, simple empirical baselines (Ridge regression at $0.09744$ and Persistence at $0.09586$) outperform all constrained deep networks, demonstrating that external physical penalties cannot uniformly compensate for deep parameter overfitting in low-sample regimes; the high competitiveness of unconstrained linear baselines underscores that deep physics constraints must be rigorously justified by operational risk reduction rather than assumed to outperform simpler models.
