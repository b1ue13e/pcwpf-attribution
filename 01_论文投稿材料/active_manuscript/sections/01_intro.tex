\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

In electric power systems and wholesale markets, the operational value of wind power forecasting is determined by asymmetric decision losses in forward scheduling and balancing settlement \cite{holttinen2021,weron2014}. Under standard balancing rules, shortfall penalties for under-delivery often differ substantially from spill revenues for over-delivery. In these asymmetric operational regimes, conventional symmetric regression metrics, such as Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) and Mean Absolute Error (MAE), fail to reflect downside financial and system reliability exposure \cite{lipton2019,pinson2007trading}. To improve generalization and enforce physically realistic outputs, an expanding literature embeds aerodynamic limits, empirical power curves, and kinematic equations directly into deep neural network architectures and loss functions \cite{raissi2019physics,karniadakis2021physics,sheng2025annreview,pct2026}. A foundational premise underpins this research paradigm: that reported reductions in forecasting error can be directly attributed to the valid physical domain knowledge encoded within the constraint.

However, prevailing reporting conventions, software availability deficits, and ablation designs obscure this attributional premise. First, contemporary studies predominantly document positive outcomes across nominal test conditions; empirical failure regimes, operational trade-offs, and constraint breakdowns remain largely undocumented. Second, public source code availability remains rare in this domain, leaving the mathematical fidelity of published loss formulations and computational graph executions largely uninspected \cite{kapoor2023,pineau2021}. Third, when ablation studies are reported, they frequently excise multi-layer neural submodules (such as graph neural network branches or attention blocks) rather than toggling the constraint penalty while holding network capacity constant. Such architectural ablations inevitably confound aerodynamic inductive bias with parametric representation capacity. Consequently, the literature leaves central empirical questions unresolved: Does a physical penalty succeed because the network internalizes genuine aerodynamic relationships, or because the penalty merely acts as a non-specific numerical regularizer? Under what training sample budgets, temporal proxy delays, and evaluation metrics do these empirical advantages persist, diminish, or invert?

To examine the attributional basis of physics-constrained gains, we conduct an empirical audit across three commercial UK wind farms (Kelmarsh, Hill of Towie, and Penmanshiel). Holding the forecasting backbone and post-hoc quantile calibration constant, we introduce form- and weight-matched pseudo-scrambled priors as negative controls, systematically perturb prior parameters, and evaluate model behavior across sample sizes. The controlled experiments reveal that the decision value of physical constraints is contingent on data volume, prior alignment, and metric selection:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Prior Specificity under Sample Scarcity}: On Penmanshiel under restricted training data ($N_{\text{train}}=1{,}583$), embedding an authentic empirical power curve prior reduces normalized asymmetric decision loss by 1.22\%. Conversely, permuting the wind-to-power mapping via a pseudo-scrambled control inflates decision loss by 68.1\%, demonstrating prior specificity under strong regularization in low-sample regimes.
\item \textbf{Diminishing Advantages under Sample Expansion}: When evaluated across training sample budgets, fixed historical empirical priors provide modest dispatch cost reductions under constrained training windows ($N_{\text{train}} \le 1{,}000$). However, because the prior is estimated from the full pre-cutoff historical record, it introduces an auxiliary seasonal data budget. As the model training window expands to $N_{\text{train}} = 5{,}000$, this prior advantage diminishes and slightly reverses, where rigid historical curves constrain the model from fitting shifting local dynamics.
\item \textbf{Software Implementation Divergence and Settlement Decoupling}: Computational graph inspection of an open-source constraint implementation reveals an inverted parameter argument in Jensen--Shannon divergence loss, altering backpropagated gradient scales by approximately 2.5 times. Downstream, market settlement analysis demonstrates that standalone unhedged balancing cashflows decouple from forecast quality: a degenerate zero-bid baseline ($q \equiv 0$) mechanically generates six-figure apparent cashflows purely through uncontracted physical spill, confirming that standalone unhedged imbalance accounting cannot serve as a reliable proxy for forecasting skill.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Summary of Contributions}
This investigation addresses the attribution and evaluation of physics-constrained forecasting through three interconnected contributions:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Systematic Scoping Audit of Literature and Code Availability}: We evaluate 46 contemporary studies (2023--2026) under PRISMA-ScR guidelines, quantifying the reporting absence of operational failure regimes (0/46), the open-code deficit (44/46), and the prevalence of structural module-level ablation confounding (23/46).
\item \textbf{Backbone-Matched Controlled Empirical Ablation}: By pairing authentic priors with scrambled negative controls across multi-seed evaluations on three commercial wind farms, we isolate physical inductive bias from non-specific numerical smoothing. We delineate the operational boundaries under which physical constraints assist, become indistinguishable from random regularization, or degrade downstream decision performance.
\item \textbf{Computational Graph Audit and Settlement Decoupling Diagnostics}: We uncover implementation divergence in public constraint codebases and demonstrate why unhedged single-settlement imbalance cashflows decouple from forecast quality, establishing an evaluation framework anchored to normalized asymmetric decision loss.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Empirical Scope and Methodological Boundaries}
This investigation operates within four defined empirical boundaries: first, the literature audit evaluates reporting and ablation practices across the 46 studies identified under our prespecified PRISMA-ScR protocol (38 journal articles and 8 preprints, 2023--2026), characterizing reporting patterns within this scoping cohort rather than asserting universal claims across unbounded keyword taxonomies; second, the software audit inspects the computational graph of the open-source PCT implementation; third, empirical ablations evaluate boundary behaviors across three commercial UK wind farms under matched DLinear backbones, with PatchTST serving as an architectural sensitivity probe; and fourth, downstream evaluations analyze operational decision risk and stylized imbalance cashflow diagnostics under UK BSC settlement prices, characterizing metric failure modes rather than full commercial portfolio clearing.
