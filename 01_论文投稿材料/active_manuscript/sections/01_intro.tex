\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

In electric power systems and spot markets, the operational value of wind power forecasting is ultimately determined by asymmetric decision losses in day-ahead commitment and balancing dispatch \cite{holttinen2021,weron2014}. Under dual-settlement balancing rules, under-delivery penalties typically diverge sharply from over-delivery spill revenues. In these asymmetric operational regimes, conventional symmetric regression criteria such as Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) or Mean Absolute Error (MAE) fail to represent downside financial and system reliability exposure \cite{lipton2019}. To improve generalization and prevent physically anomalous outputs, a surging body of literature embeds aerodynamic limits, empirical turbine power curves, or kinematic equations directly into deep neural network architectures and loss functions \cite{raissi2019physics,karniadakis2021physics,sheng2025annreview,pct2026}. A foundational premise underpins this research paradigm: that reported reductions in forecasting error can be directly attributed to the valid domain knowledge encoded within the physical constraint.

However, prevailing reporting conventions, software availability deficits, and ablation designs obscure this attributional premise. First, across contemporary literature, published studies predominantly document positive outcomes; empirical failures, negative trade-offs, or constraint breakdowns remain virtually absent. Second, public source code availability remains exceptionally rare, leaving computational graph executions unverified \cite{kapoor2023,pineau2021}. Third, where ablation studies are reported, they frequently excise entire multi-layer neural submodules (such as dual-channel encoders or dynamic attention blocks) rather than isolating the physical penalty parameter holding model capacity constant. Such coarse ablations inevitably confound aerodynamic inductive bias with parametric representation capacity. Consequently, the literature leaves fundamental questions unresolved: does a physical penalty succeed because the network internalizes genuine aerodynamic relationships, or because the penalty merely acts as a non-specific numerical regularizer? Under what training sample budgets, temporal proxy lags, and evaluation metrics do these empirical advantages persist, degrade, or invert?

To examine the attributional basis of physics-constrained gains, we conduct a controlled empirical audit across three commercial UK wind farms (Kelmarsh, Hill of Towie, and Penmanshiel). Holding the forecasting backbone (DLinear) and post-hoc asymmetric quantile calibration strictly identical, we introduce scale-matched pseudo-scrambled priors as negative controls, systematically perturb prior parameters, and vary model training horizons. The controlled experiments reveal that physical constraint advantages are bounded and contingent:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Prior Specificity vs. Misspecification Fragility}: On Penmanshiel under restricted training samples ($N_{\text{train}}=1{,}583$), a strong power curve constraint reduces normalized asymmetric decision loss by 1.22\%. However, permuting the wind-speed-to-power mapping while preserving loss formulation and gradient magnitude inflates decision loss by 68.1\%. This contrast confirms that while valid domain information can provide measurable inductive advantage in data-scarce settings, it introduces substantial vulnerability when the prior is misspecified.
\item \textbf{Information Budgets and Sample Regimes}: When models are evaluated under varying training sample budgets, fixed historical empirical priors provide modest benefits under limited training windows (e.g., $N=500$, representing $\sim 21$ days of recent operations). However, because the prior is estimated from the full historical training span (7 to 10 months), it injects significant additional information. As the model's own training window expands to $N=5{,}000$, this prior advantage diminishes and eventually reverses, where rigid historical curves constrain the model from fitting shifting local dynamics.
\item \textbf{Implementation Divergence and Market Metric Decoupling}: At the software execution level, an inspection of open-source computational graphs identifies an inverted parameter argument in the Jensen--Shannon divergence loss of the Physics-Constrained Transformer (PCT), which induces asymmetric gradient amplification. Downstream, an analysis of market settlement demonstrates that standalone unhedged balancing cashflow is structurally decoupled from forecasting skill: a degenerate zero-bid strategy ($\hat{y} \equiv 0$) mechanically accrues substantial apparent cashflow despite generating zero predictive information.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Summary of Contributions}
This audit addresses the attribution and evaluation of physics-constrained forecasting through three interconnected contributions:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Scoping Audit of Literature Reporting and Code Availability}: We evaluate 46 contemporary studies (2023--2026) under PRISMA-ScR guidelines, quantifying the reporting absence of operational failure regimes (0/46, 0.0\%), the deficit of open-source artifacts (44/46, 95.7\%), and the prevalence of structural module-level ablation confounding (23/46, 50.0\%).
\item \textbf{Backbone-Matched Controlled Empirical Ablation}: By pairing authentic priors with scrambled negative controls across 3--4 independent seeds per wind farm on a dedicated 10-GPU cluster, we isolate domain-specific inductive bias from numerical smoothing. We delineate the operational boundaries under which physical constraints assist, become indistinguishable from random regularization, or degrade decision performance.
\item \textbf{Computational Graph and Settlement Decoupling Diagnostics}: We uncover implementation divergence in public constraint software and mathematically resolve why unhedged single-settlement imbalance cashflows fail as a surrogate for forecast quality, establishing a decision-theoretic audit framework anchored to normalized asymmetric dispatch loss.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Empirical Scope and Methodological Boundaries}
This investigation defines four explicit operational boundaries: first, the scoping literature audit evaluates reporting and ablation practices across the 46 contemporary studies identified under our PRISMA-ScR protocol (38 peer-reviewed journal articles and 8 preprints, 2023--2026), characterizing reporting gaps within this scoping cohort rather than universal disciplinary properties; second, the software audit examines the computational graph of the open-source PCT implementation (commit \texttt{65941ec}); third, the empirical ablations characterize boundary behavior across three commercial UK wind farms under matched DLinear backbones with PatchTST serving as an architectural sensitivity probe; and fourth, the downstream evaluations conduct an operational decision-risk and single-settlement imbalance cashflow diagnostic under asymmetric two-settlement rules, establishing metric failure modes rather than full commercial portfolio clearing. Together, these four components establish an evidence-grounded assessment from mathematical specification to market dispatch.
