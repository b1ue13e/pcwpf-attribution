\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Three findings from this audit challenge the prevailing consensus on physics-constrained wind power forecasting.

Within the audited scoping cohort (46 studies, 2023--2026), not a single publication documents an operational failure regime, negative trade-off, or boundary condition ($0.0\%$). With 95.7\% of the cohort lacking public code and 50.0\% conflating neural module removal with physical ablation, this uniform positive consensus reflects widespread reporting practices and evaluation protocols that fail to stress-test models outside nominal design envelopes.

At the software implementation level, of the two publicly accessible software repositories identified across the 46 studies, PINN-UQ was verified as code-aligned, while PCT---the sole publicly accessible training codebase for end-to-end physics-constrained Transformers---executes its Jensen--Shannon divergence loss with reversed tensor arguments that induce an analytical inverse-density gradient singularity ($\mathcal{O}(1/\epsilon)$), an implementation divergence undetectable from published text alone. Algebraic derivation separately confirms that the paired kernel product remains mathematically sound, establishing that implementation fidelity and formulation validity reside in distinct computational layers.

At the empirical evaluation level, the decision value of physical constraints depends strictly on sample volume, proxy temporal alignment, and market settlement metrics. At $N_{\text{train}} = 1{,}583$ and $\lambda = 1.0$, an authentic power curve prior reduces normalized dispatch cost by 1.22\%; a form- and weight-matched scrambled prior raises it by 68.1\%, demonstrating acute sensitivity to prior misspecification under strong regularization. Weak constraints ($\lambda = 0.01$) yield outcomes comparable to structurally scrambled negative controls, behaving empirically like non-specific numerical smoothing, while proxy observation staleness in the training objective ($k > 12$) inflates dispatch costs across all three operational wind farms.

The PCT implementation divergence, the Zero-Bid settlement anomaly, and the scrambled-prior degradation reflect a shared methodological vulnerability: standard evaluation protocols test physics-constrained models without distinguishing physical inductive bias from non-specific numerical smoothing, software execution fidelity from published equations, or forecasting skill from market settlement structures. Auditing constraint provenance, software execution, telemetry latency, and coupled forward and balancing cashflows provides the empirical foundation necessary to determine when domain physical priors deliver genuine operational value in power systems.

In our tested configurations, empirical power-curve priors provide measurable inductive value under restricted training windows, but their operational benefits require rigorous empirical boundaries. Establishing those boundaries demands parameter-isolated ablations, scrambled negative controls, static computational graph verification, and market evaluation protocols that separate forward commitment revenue from imbalance settlement cashflow.
