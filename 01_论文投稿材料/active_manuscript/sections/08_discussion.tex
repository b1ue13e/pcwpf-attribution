\section{Discussion, Conceptual Synthesis, and Reporting Governance}
\label{sec:discussion}

In this section, we synthesize our empirical and theoretical findings into a conceptual bias--variance framework, explicitly delineate what our controlled experiments formally identify versus what remains unmeasured, document falsified initial conjectures, and formulate an actionable operational governance protocol.

\subsection{Conceptual Bias--Variance Synthesis: Understanding the Operating Envelope}
The empirical patterns mapped across our benchmarks can be conceptually interpreted through the trade-off between variance reduction and structural approximation bias, serving strictly as conceptual interpretations consistent with---not identified by---the observed learning curves:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Tested Sample-Restricted Regimes ($N_{\text{train}} \in \{500, 1{,}000\}$)}: Under a conceptual variance-reduction interpretation, data-driven forecasters in sample-restricted regimes are hypothesized to suffer from elevated parameter estimation variance, rendering them prone to non-physical overfitting. Under strong regularization ($\lambda=1.0$), an authentic empirical power curve constrains the hypothesis space to meteorologically plausible manifolds, providing substantial downside risk reduction ($-1.28\%$ on Penmanshiel, $-2.00\%$ on Kelmarsh, $-3.62\%$ on Hill of Towie). However, if the prior is misspecified or permuted, this rigid constraint enforces counter-physical parameter updates, triggering severe performance degradation ($+61.2\%$).
\item \textbf{Largest Evaluated Training Budget ($N_{\text{train}} = 5{,}000$)}: Purely data-driven representations can fit the observed input--output relationship more flexibly as training coverage expands from operational telemetry. Conceptually, any variance-reduction benefit offered by a fixed prior is hypothesized to diminish relative to emerging structural approximation bias inherent in a static, one-dimensional empirical curve. At the largest evaluated training budget ($N_{\text{train}}=5{,}000$), unconstrained DLinear matches or slightly outperforms the fixed prior (+0.33\% on Kelmarsh, +1.73\% on Hill of Towie).
\item \textbf{Penalty Scaling and Numerical Smoothing}: Under weak regularization ($\lambda=0.01$), genuine physical priors and scrambled pseudo-priors yield closely matched performance. This lack of empirical separation demonstrates that weak constraints function primarily as non-specific numerical regularizers, challenging the assumption that marginal gains under small penalty weights represent physical understanding.
\end{enumerate}
We emphasize that this bias--variance perspective serves strictly as a \textbf{conceptual interpretation} consistent with---not identified or proven by---the observed learning curves, rather than an empirically measured algebraic identity. Taken together, our learning-curve and penalty-sensitivity experiments suggest that both data availability and regularization strength jointly govern the operating envelope of physical priors.

\subsection{Attributional Scope: What Is Evaluated vs.\ What Is Not}
\label{sec:identification}
To preserve methodological clarity, we explicitly distinguish the attributional scope established by our experiments from unresolved confounding factors:

\paragraph{Empirically Distinguished Under the Controlled Protocol}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, label=(\alph*)]
\item \textbf{Domain Specificity under Strong Regularization}: Under the tested matched-control protocol, divergence between authentic and pseudo-scrambled priors provides evidence that the observed performance shift is specific to the empirical aerodynamic mapping, rather than being explained by penalty form and weight alone.
\item \textbf{Decoupling from Historical Information Budgets}: The strictly budget-matched information ablation ($N_{\text{prior}}=N_{\text{train}}=500$) confirms that domain-structured prior effects retain 77\% to 92\% of the full-history mean benefit, demonstrating that the majority of the observed gain cannot be explained by auxiliary historical data volume alone.
\item \textbf{Attenuating Returns across Training Volumes}: Subsampled within-site learning curves show that prior advantages diminish as training volume expands, reversing slightly under the evaluated strong-prior setting at $N_{\text{train}}=5{,}000$.
\item \textbf{Vulnerability to Prior Distortion and Latency}: Continuous corruption scans identify an empirical crossover region around $\alpha \approx 0.07$ on Penmanshiel, while the Hill of Towie lag scan reveals that decision risk exceeds the unconstrained reference by the first evaluated lag $k=6$ (1\,h delay) and deteriorates monotonically through $k=36$ when training losses rely on stale historical weather proxies. These values are empirical crossover locations along the evaluated perturbation grid, not population-level physical thresholds.
\item \textbf{Prediction--Decision Decoupling}: The Info-Symmetric benchmark confirms that reducing RMSE can simultaneously worsen asymmetric dispatch loss, and the Zero-Bid reductio ad absurdum proves that standalone unhedged balancing cashflow is structurally decoupled from forecasting skill.
\item \textbf{Software Discrepancy in Divergence Losses}: Static AST analysis and gradient derivations identify an analytical $\mathcal{O}(1/\epsilon)$ gradient singularity in the open-source PCT codebase, confirming that implementation divergence can alter optimization trajectories.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{What Is Not Identified (Confounding Disclaimers)}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, label=(\roman*)]
\item \textbf{Cross-Site Topographical Causation}: Differences across Kelmarsh, Hill of Towie, and Penmanshiel confound topographic complexity with turbine ratings (2.05\,MW vs.\ 2.30\,MW), hub heights, wake geometry, and local micro-climates. We do not assert that complex terrain causally mandates physical constraints; topography remains an observational covariate.
\item \textbf{Universal Causal Effect of Physics}: Our experiments evaluate specific empirical power-curve and distributional constraints under evaluated configurations; they do not establish universal causal claims for ``physics'' as an abstract treatment across all energy systems.
\item \textbf{Global Generalization of Auditing Metrics}: The 95.7\% code deficit and 50.0\% confounded ablation rates characterize the prespecified 46-study scoping cohort (2023--2026), rather than all applied AI literature. Similarly, the absence of documented failure regimes describes the reporting scope of the surveyed publications (which reported exclusively within nominal operating envelopes) rather than identifying underlying researcher behavior or file-drawer effects.
\item \textbf{Universal Software Defects}: Demonstrating an implementation divergence in PCT confirms that code-manuscript mismatches occur, but does not imply that all physics-informed forecasting codebases contain errors.
\item \textbf{Universality of Decoupled Calibration}: Counterexamples 1 and 2 are formal existence proofs establishing theoretical limits under spatial heteroscedasticity, not an empirical claim that scalar calibration universally underperforms joint training.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Two Conjectures Falsified During the Audit}
Documenting falsified initial hypotheses provides an auditable record of our inquiry:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textit{Bivariate Coupling in Tensor Outer Products}: We initially hypothesized that the tensor product $K_p^\top K_w$ in PCT assumed statistical independence ($p(w, P) = p(w)p(P)$). Formal algebraic expansion (Equation~\eqref{eq:kde_proof}) refuted this conjecture, proving that contracting over batch samples preserves authentic bivariate product-kernel KDE coupling.
\item \textit{Price Asymmetry Dominance over Prior Regularization}: We initially conjectured that physical distribution priors would consistently improve balancing market cashflows across all sites. Dynamic settlement analysis refuted this hypothesis: distribution priors reduced shortfall costs on Hill of Towie while increasing shortfall penalties on Kelmarsh, demonstrating that price spike timing dominates physical regularizers in unhedged cashflow calculations.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Actionable Guidance: The Five-Step Practical Decision Rule}
Before deploying physical priors into operational forecasting systems, engineers and system operators should evaluate five sequential criteria:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Prior Specificity Check}: Does the authentic physical prior outperform a form- and weight-matched scrambled negative control under the planned regularization weight? If genuine and scrambled priors yield closely matched outcomes, the observed gain should be treated as non-specific numerical smoothing.
\item \textbf{Information Budget Alignment Check}: When fitted strictly on the available training sample window without auxiliary historical archives, does the prior retain the majority of its performance advantage? If not, the apparent gain is attributable to auxiliary historical data volume rather than inductive physical guidance.
\item \textbf{Sample Volume and Regularization Annealing Check}: As training data expands, is the regularization weight $\lambda$ systematically annealed? In well-sampled regimes, rigid constraints should be relaxed to mitigate potential structural approximation bias from degrading predictions.
\item \textbf{State Alignment and Latency Tolerance Check}: Does the physical prior rely on lagged meteorological proxies? In operational deployment, stress tests must verify whether the prior's decision advantage survives expected telemetry and NWP delivery latencies.
\item \textbf{Decision-Aligned Metric Check}: Is the prior evaluated against asymmetric operational dispatch risk ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{disp}}$ at the operational penalty ratio) rather than symmetric point errors (RMSE) or unhedged balancing cashflows? Models that reduce RMSE while elevating shortfall risk should be barred from deployment.
\end{enumerate}
