\begin{abstract}
Reductions in point forecasting error from physical constraints do not establish that empirical gains stem from valid physical information, nor do they guarantee operational decision value. In the published literature, physical constraints are widely credited with improving renewable energy forecasts, but existing evaluation protocols rarely isolate domain physical guidance from parametric capacity, audit software execution fidelity, or measure asymmetric operational costs. We audit the attributional foundation of physics-constrained wind power forecasting through systematic literature scoping ($N=46$), static software code inspection, and prospective controlled ablations across three commercial UK wind farms. Within the scoped cohort (2023--2026), 0.0\% of studies document operational failure boundaries, 95.7\% lack public source code, and 50.0\% evaluate constraints via confounded module excisions. Software inspection of the open-source PCT benchmark uncovers an inverted argument in its divergence loss that induces an analytical gradient singularity, though its bivariate kernel coupling remains mathematically sound. In controlled experiments holding the neural backbone and post-hoc quantile calibration constant, strong empirical power curve constraints ($\lambda=1.0$) reduce normalized asymmetric decision loss by 1.22\% under sample restriction (Penmanshiel), whereas form- and weight-matched scrambled priors inflate loss by 68.1\%, demonstrating prior specificity under data scarcity. Conversely, weak penalties ($\lambda=0.01$) mimic non-specific numerical smoothing, while offline historical weather proxy staleness ($k > 12$) monotonically degrades performance across all sites. Finally, degenerate zero-bid probing demonstrates that standalone unhedged balancing cashflows decouple from forecast quality under forward-contracted wholesale accounting, indicating that verifiable physical forecasting claims require backbone-matched negative controls, disclosed data budgets, and decision-aligned evaluation.
\end{abstract}
