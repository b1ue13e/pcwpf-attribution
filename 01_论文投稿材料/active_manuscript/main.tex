\begin{abstract}
Reductions in point forecasting error from physical constraints do not establish that empirical gains stem from valid physical information, nor do they guarantee operational decision value. We audit the attributional foundation of physics-constrained wind power forecasting through systematic literature scoping, software code inspection, and multi-site controlled experiments across three commercial UK wind farms. Among 46 scoped studies (2023--2026), zero report operational failure regimes or trade-offs, 44 lack accessible source code, and 23 evaluate constraints by excising multi-layer network modules, confounding physical inductive bias with parametric capacity. In controlled ablations holding the forecasting backbone and post-hoc quantile calibration constant, the impact of empirical power curve priors varies across data regimes: on Penmanshiel, strong constraints reduce normalized asymmetric decision loss by 1.22\%, whereas destroying the aerodynamic mapping via pseudo-scrambled controls inflates decision loss by 68.1\%, demonstrating prior specificity under data scarcity. Across well-sampled sites, fixed historical priors provide marginal loss reductions under constrained training windows, an advantage that diminishes and reverses as model training sample sizes expand. Software code inspection reveals an inverted parameter argument in an official divergence loss implementation that alters gradient scales, while market settlement diagnostics demonstrate that standalone unhedged balancing cashflows decouple from forecast quality under degenerate zero-bid bidding. These findings indicate that empirical claims of physical benefit require backbone-matched negative controls, transparent disclosure of prior historical data budgets, and consistent decision-theoretic evaluation.
\end{abstract}
